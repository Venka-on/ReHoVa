What to Bring to Your Showroom Visit

A simple checklist to help make your renovation discussion more useful.

Showroom

A showroom visit is more useful when you can relate the samples and ideas to your actual space. You do not need to have every detail decided in advance. A few basic references can help make the discussion more focused and help the renovation team understand what you are trying to achieve.

A simple preparation checklist

  • Floor plan or rough measurements: Bring a plan if you have one. Note the room dimensions and any important openings or fixed features, but treat rough measurements as a starting point for discussion.
  • Photos of the space: Include wide views and details of existing items that need to stay, such as windows, doors, columns or plumbing points.
  • Inspiration: Save a few reference images and point out what you like in each one, such as storage, colour, lighting or layout.
  • Existing finishes: Photos or samples of flooring, countertops and paint can make it easier to compare colours and textures.
  • Your priorities: List the main problems you want to solve, who will use the space and which features matter most.
  • Budget and timing: Share a comfortable budget range and any target dates so the proposed scope can be discussed realistically.

Questions worth asking

Ask what information is needed for a detailed quotation, what the proposed scope includes, which selections are still open, and how revisions are handled. If you are comparing providers, bring the other quotation or scope details so you can check whether you are comparing equivalent work.
