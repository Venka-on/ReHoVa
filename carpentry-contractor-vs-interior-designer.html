Direct Carpentry Contractor or Interior Designer?

How to compare project scope, responsibilities and quotations for a renovation.

Planning

There is no single right route for every renovation. The best fit depends on how much of the project involves custom carpentry, how much design and coordination support you need, and which responsibilities are included in the proposed scope. Titles alone do not tell you exactly what a provider will deliver, so compare the work and responsibilities in writing.

Understand the different scopes

An interior designer may help develop the overall look and layout and coordinate a broader range of renovation work, depending on the agreement. A direct carpentry provider may be a more focused option when the main requirement is custom cabinetry or built-in storage. Some businesses combine design, carpentry and other renovation services, so confirm the actual scope rather than assuming it from the company label.

For a carpentry-focused project, clarify who will measure the site, prepare drawings, confirm materials, coordinate access, manage changes and handle installation. For a wider renovation, identify who coordinates work between trades and how decisions or site issues will be communicated.

Compare quotations fairly

  • Check that each quotation describes the same rooms, quantities, materials and finish details.
  • Ask what is included and excluded, including delivery, installation and any related work.
  • Confirm how measurements, drawings and final selections are approved before work proceeds.
  • Ask how changes are priced and approved, and who is your main contact during the project.

A lower headline price may reflect a narrower scope, so compare the itemised details before deciding. Choose an arrangement that matches the complexity of your project and gives you a clear understanding of who is responsible for each part.
